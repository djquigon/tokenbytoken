## 1. Why is the sky blue?

The sky looks blue because of **Rayleigh scattering**. Tiny molecules in Earth’s atmosphere (like nitrogen and oxygen) scatter sunlight, but **blue light is scattered much more** than red light because it has a shorter wavelength. Sunlight also contains all visible colors; when these are scattered, the blue component reaches your eyes from many parts of the sky across the daytime. At sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels through more atmosphere, so more blue light gets scattered away and the remaining light looks **more orange or red**.

## 3. How do vaccines work?

Vaccines train your immune system to recognize and fight a specific germ (like a virus or bacteria). They contain a harmless piece of the germ, a weakened germ, or instructions for making a harmless part of it. Your body responds by making antibodies and “memory” immune cells. Later, if you’re exposed to the real germ, those memory cells quickly recognize it and mount a faster, stronger response—often preventing illness or making it much milder. This is why vaccines protect individuals and help reduce spread in communities.

## 5. What's the difference between weather and climate?

**Weather** is what the atmosphere is doing *right now* or over a short period (hours to days). Example: “It’s raining today” or “It will be sunny tomorrow.”

**Climate** is the *average pattern of weather* over a long time (typically 30 years or more). It includes typical temperature ranges, rainfall patterns, and seasonal changes. Example: “This region has a Mediterranean climate—wet winters and dry summers.”

So, weather changes day to day; climate describes long-term trends.

## 10. How does a refrigerator keep food cold?

A refrigerator keeps food cold by moving heat out of the inside. It uses a refrigerant (a special fluid) that cycles through a sealed system. First, the compressor pressurizes the refrigerant, making it hot. Then the refrigerant flows to the condenser coils outside or at the back, where it releases heat into the room. Next, the refrigerant goes through an expansion valve, where it rapidly expands and becomes cold. Finally, it runs through the evaporator coils inside the fridge, absorbing heat from the food and air—so the inside gets cold. The cycle repeats continuously.

## 13. Explain recursion to a 12-year-old.

Recursion is when a problem is solved by repeating a smaller version of the same problem. Imagine you’re counting from 1 to 3: to count to 3, you count to 2 first, then say “3.”  

A good recursion has:  
1) **Base case**: a simple stop condition (so it doesn’t go forever). For example, “If you just need to count to 1, stop.”  
2) **Recursive step**: the part that calls itself with a smaller problem.  

Example: Factorial of 4 = 4 × factorial of 3. And factorial of 3 depends on factorial of 2, until you reach factorial of 1.

## 14. What causes the seasons on Earth?

Earth’s seasons are caused mainly by the tilt of Earth’s axis. Earth is tilted about 23.5° relative to its orbit around the Sun. As Earth travels around the Sun, different parts of Earth receive more direct sunlight and for longer (summer), or less direct sunlight and for shorter (winter). This change in sunlight affects day length and the intensity of solar energy, which leads to seasonal temperature differences.  

Note: It’s not primarily because Earth is closer or farther from the Sun—Earth’s distance changes slightly, but the tilt has the biggest impact.
